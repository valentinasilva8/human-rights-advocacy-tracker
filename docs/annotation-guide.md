# Annotation guide

**Status: provisional. Needs legal-mentor review before it is used on real cases.**

This guide is for people and models who propose records in Advocacy Trace. It is not a universal theory of freedom of expression. The five labels overlap. One argument can carry more than one label. One case can contain several arguments.

Reference for mentor review, not reproduced here: [Human Rights Committee, General Comment No. 34](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/english/bodies/hrc/docs/gc34.pdf). Preserve the authority and reasoning the source actually uses.

Headline labels, used exactly:

1. Vagueness
2. Broadness
3. Legality
4. Necessity
5. Proportionality

Anything else is stored as `unmapped—review required`. Do not invent a sixth headline category.

## Shared rules

Write: "The report argues that the restriction is disproportionate because…"

Do not write: "The AI determines that the restriction violates proportionality."

Record the author. A report can describe counsel's argument, a partner's filing, or an authority's finding. Those are not TrialWatch's reasoning unless the document is advancing that reasoning in its own voice.

A keyword, a background quotation, or the prosecution's argument is not an extraction.

If the available text is only an announcement about a brief, set the attribution role to announcement-only. Do not imply that the full brief was read.

Keep the supporting passage and a page, paragraph, or other stable location. An approved argument cannot omit them.

If the fit is uncertain, use `unmapped—review required` or attach more than one of the five labels and say why. Uncertainty stays visible.

## Vagueness

The source argues that the rule does not say clearly enough what is prohibited, or that officials have excessive discretion to decide what it covers.

Positive example (synthetic): a report argues that an offense turning on speech "harmful to public calm," with that phrase undefined, leaves people unable to know what is forbidden.

Exclude: a claim that the sentence was too long. That may be proportionality. Exclude a quotation of the statute that the source does not itself criticize.

## Broadness

The source argues that the restriction reaches protected expression beyond the scope that could be justified.

Positive example (synthetic): a report argues that a ban on "criticism of officials" covers political commentary that has no stated link to violence or fraud.

Exclude: a claim only that the wording is hard to understand. That may be vagueness. A law can be both broad and vague; if the source makes both points, use both labels.

## Legality

The source argues about the legal basis of a restriction, including whether the rule is accessible or foreseeable, or whether the prosecution relies on a provision that is not in force.

Positive example (synthetic): a report argues that the court applied a criminal provision the legislature had already repealed.

Exclude: a policy argument that the law is unwise but still clearly on the books. That may belong elsewhere, or may be unmapped.

## Necessity

The source challenges the asserted need for the restriction, or the connection between the expression and a specific threat the state names.

Positive example (synthetic): a report argues that the state invoked public order without identifying any concrete risk tied to the post.

Exclude: a claim that some response might be needed but that prison is too severe. The severity point may be proportionality.

## Proportionality

The source challenges the scope or severity of a restriction relative to its aim. Less restrictive alternatives count when the source discusses them.

Positive example (synthetic): a report argues that a two-year prison sentence for one satirical post is excessive, and that the court was not shown a correction or a civil remedy.

Exclude: the mere fact of a conviction or an acquittal. The outcome is a separate record. Exclude a court's recital of the argument unless the decision adopts, rejects, or otherwise resolves it. A recital is not acceptance.

## Overlap

Necessity and proportionality often appear together. Label both when the source makes both points, and keep the reasons distinct. Vagueness and broadness can appear together. Do not collapse them into a single "speech" tag.

## Uncertainty

- Partial or missing dates stay partial or missing.
- A model suggestion is stored as AI-suggested and `proposed`, never as an approved TrialWatch argument, until a person reviews it.
- Do not force a poor fit into the five labels.

## Mentor review still required

Before real cases are coded, a legal mentor should confirm the examples, the exclusions, and the decision to keep fair-trial issues out of this headline taxonomy.
